# Data quality report

_Generated by `python -m engine.explore_data` — do not edit by hand; re-run the script._

Dataset: Kaggle **"Solar Power Generation Data"** by anikannal — two solar plants in India. Plant location (GPS) is **not** in the dataset.

A 15-minute slot counts as **daylight** when irradiation > 0.02 (`DAYLIGHT_IRRADIATION_THRESHOLD` in config).

## 2. What every column means

| Column | File | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `DATE_TIME` | All files | When the reading was taken. One reading every 15 minutes. |
| `PLANT_ID` | All files | Which solar plant (4135001 = Plant 1, 4136001 = Plant 2). |
| `SOURCE_KEY` | Generation | The ID of one **inverter** — the box that turns the panels' DC electricity into AC electricity for the grid. ~22 per plant. This is what we monitor. |
| `SOURCE_KEY` | Weather | The ID of the plant's single weather sensor (not an inverter). |
| `DC_POWER` | Generation | Power (kW) coming **in** from the solar panels. DC = direct current, like a battery. |
| `AC_POWER` | Generation | Power (kW) going **out** to the grid after the inverter. AC = alternating current, like a wall socket. Should be a little less than DC (small conversion loss). |
| `DAILY_YIELD` | Generation | Energy (kWh) the inverter has produced **so far today**. Should start at 0 each morning and only go up during the day. |
| `TOTAL_YIELD` | Generation | Energy (kWh) produced over the inverter's **whole life**. Should only ever go up. |
| `AMBIENT_TEMPERATURE` | Weather | Air temperature around the plant (°C). |
| `MODULE_TEMPERATURE` | Weather | Temperature of the solar panels themselves (°C). Hot panels make a bit less power. |
| `IRRADIATION` | Weather | Strength of the sunlight hitting the panels (about kW per m²). 0 at night, around 1 at a sunny noon. More sunlight = more power. |

## 4. Issues found and how we handle them

| Issue | Size (measured) | How we handle it |
|---|---|---|
| Plant 1 DC_POWER is about 10× too large | Median AC÷DC in daylight: Plant 1 = 0.0979, Plant 2 = 0.9787. Plant 1 keeps only ~9.8% of DC, which is impossible → Plant 1 DC is ~10.0× too big. **Quirk confirmed.** | Phase 2: divide Plant 1 DC by a factor computed from the data. Labelled ASSUMPTION. |
| Missing inverter readings | Plant 1: 4.22% missing. Plant 2: 5.72% missing; worst inverter misses 28.3% of daylight. | Phase 2: keep gaps as explicit rows (`is_missing = True`), never fill with fake power. Phase 4: raise a **Data gap / communication loss** alert. |
| Missing weather timestamps | Plant 1: 82, Plant 2: 5. | Phase 2: no sunlight value → we cannot compute expected power there; mark and skip. |
| AC greater than DC | Plant 1: 0 rows. Plant 2: 18 rows, at most 0.83% above DC. | Tiny measurement noise, not a real fault. Keep the rows; count them in Phase 2. |
| Negative power or sunlight | Power: P1 0, P2 0. Irradiation: P1 0, P2 0. | Nothing to fix. Phase 2 tests will keep checking. |
| Sunlight at night (20:00–05:00) | Plant 1: 0 rows. Plant 2: 47 rows, max 0.0029. | Tiny sensor noise, far below our daylight threshold (0.02), so it is automatically treated as night. |
| DAILY_YIELD does not start the day at 0 | Plant 1: 205 of 748 inverter-days. Plant 2: 530 of 716. Of these, 161 (P1) and 516 (P2) are the 00:00 reading still showing yesterday's total (the counter resets a bit late). | We do **not** use DAILY_YIELD for energy. We compute energy ourselves: AC_POWER × 0.25 h. |
| DAILY_YIELD goes down during the day | Drops of any kind: P1 439, P2 1888. Drops while the sun is up: P1 38, P2 951. | Same as above — the counter is unreliable, so we ignore it for energy. |
| TOTAL_YIELD is broken in Plant 2 | Plant 2: 563 readings are 0 and the counter goes down 1161 times; inverter lifetime totals range from 520,758 to 2,247,916,295 kWh. Plant 1: 0 zeros, 0 drops (fine). | We do **not** use TOTAL_YIELD. Plant-level counter problems feed the **Sensor/data issue** alert. |
