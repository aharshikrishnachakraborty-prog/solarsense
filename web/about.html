About SolarSense

AI solar plant health monitor — hackathon project for Meridian problem DEV-P01 (Application Development track).

The problem

Solar plant operators usually find out that a panel string or an inverter is underperforming only after the losses have piled up — often at the end of the month, when the energy bill looks low.

SolarSense compares what every inverter actually produced with what it should have produced for the weather at that moment, flags likely faults with a plain-English reason, and estimates the energy and money lost — so the maintenance team knows what to fix first.

How it works

The pipeline computes everything in advance; the website only displays results. The AI never decides alone — clear rules raise every alert and explain why.

Limitations (honest)

  • Only a few weeks of data from one season (May–June); accuracy in winter or monsoon is unknown.
  • One weather sensor per plant: a cloud over one corner of a plant can look like a fault there. Comparing each inverter with its neighbours reduces this.
  • No cloud sensor and no GPS: the sky (clear → overcast) is read from the sunlight against a clear-sky curve learned from the brightest days — an estimate, not a measurement. The temperature effect is measured from our data; for Plant 2 it is weak evidence.
  • No official fault labels exist, so we validated with faults we planted ourselves — see the Validation page.
  • Plant 1's DC power was rescaled because the raw values were physically impossible (see assumptions below).
  • The ₹ figures use an assumed tariff (configurable) until the team cites a sourced one — see Assumptions below.
  • Small underperformance is usually not caught, by design, to avoid false alarms.
